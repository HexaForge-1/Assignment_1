1. Which factor most strongly impacts productivity?
   Number of Projects is the strongest predictor. In a standardized model, it carries the highest weight, suggesting that the volume of active work is more indicative of
   output than time spent alone.

2. How does training affect productivity?
   Training has a positive impact. Every additional hour of training correlates with an increase in productivity, though its relative weight is lower than experience or
   projects.

3. Should the company increase training hours or working hours?
   The company should prioritize Training Hours. While both increase productivity, working hours are subject to faster diminishing returns (burnout), whereas training
   improves the efficiency of every hour worked.

4. What happens if Working Hours increase beyond optimal limits?
   Productivity begins to decrease. Our quadratic model shows a negative squared coefficient for Working Hours, indicating that after a certain point (the peak of the curve), additional hours lead to fatigue and lower total output.

5. Can productivity ever decrease with more experience?
   In this specific linear model, no. Experience shows a steady positive coefficient. However, if 'Experience' were tested for non-linearity (like Working Hours),
   we might see stagnation, but currently, more experience equals higher scores.

6. How would you detect overfitting in this model?
   By comparing Training R² vs. Cross-Validation (LOOCV) Scores. If the model performs significantly better on the 10 data points provided than on 'held-out' points during
   validation, it is overfitting (memorizing noise).

7. Suggest one new feature to improve prediction accuracy.
   'Task Complexity' or 'Tool Proficiency'. These would help explain why two employees with the same hours and experience might have different productivity scores based
    on the difficulty of the work assigned.
