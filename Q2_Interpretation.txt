1. Identify high-risk customers.
High-risk customers in our dataset are typically older individuals who are self-employed and seeking larger, long-term loans. Even if their income is high,
their lower credit scores make them statistically similar to previous defaulters.

2. What patterns lead to loan default?
A clear 'Default Cluster' appears when a Low Credit Score (<680) is combined with a Long Loan Term (10-15 years). Interestingly, income doesn't save these applicants,
the pattern suggests that credit history and the duration of the debt are more critical than monthly earnings.

3. How do credit score and income influence predictions?
In this model, Credit Score is the primary predictor. While income tells us how much a customer *could* pay, the Credit Score tells us how likely they *are* to pay.
Our analysis shows a much stronger negative correlation between Credit Score and default than the positive correlation with income.

4. Suggest banking policies based on model output.
- Self-Employed Threshold: Require a minimum Credit Score of 700 for self-employed applicants.
- Risk Capping: Limit the Loan-to-Income ratio for applicants over the age of 45 to reduce late-career default risks.

5. Compare KNN with Decision Trees for this problem.
KNN is effective here because it finds similar 'financial profiles'. However, a Decision Tree might be better for transparency, as it provides clear 'If-Then' rules (e.g., 'If Score < 680, Reject') that are easier to explain to customers than KNN's 'black box' approach.

6. What happens if LoanAmount dominates distance calculation?
Without scaling, the Loan Amount (in lakhs) would mathematically dominate the distance calculation because its values are much larger than Employment Type (0 or 1).
The model would focus almost entirely on loan size and ignore the applicant's employment stability, leading to biased results.

7. Should KNN be used in real-time loan approval systems?
KNN is excellent for real-time approvals in smaller institutions because it requires zero training time. However, for a major bank with millions of records,
KNN can become slow during the approval process because it must compare every new applicant to every existing record. For massive scale, models like Logistic Regression
or Neural Networks are more efficient.
